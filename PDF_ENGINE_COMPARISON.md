# PDF engine comparison

Primary documentation checked on 27 October 2026. Tested versions are a reproducible snapshot, not a promise to track every newest package. Capabilities marked "researched" were not exercised locally. License descriptions are engineering selection guidance; distribution requires review of the exact shipped binaries, dependencies, fonts and intended application license.

## Important practical distinctions

PDFium's `FPDFText_SetText` **does edit existing text objects**. It is inaccurate to classify PDFium as render-only. Our saved-object experiment verifies this path, but it changed an entire line object, not a universally addressable word/span. Explicit glyph offsets, missing font mappings, nested forms and object splitting still need investigation.

PyMuPDF `apply_redactions` followed by insertion is real content removal and recreation. It is not a visual covering trick, but neither is it preservation of the original text object. [The page API](https://pymupdf.readthedocs.io/en/latest/page.html) warns that redactions operate on overlapping character boxes, replacement placement can differ, and longer replacements may fit poorly. Our experiment preserves images/graphics by using explicit text-only settings and inserts separately at the captured baseline.

qpdf deliberately focuses on structural transformations, with content generation/semantics left to the caller. [Its overview](https://qpdf.readthedocs.io/en/stable/overview.html) supports this distinction. Our successful pikepdf mutations are custom fixture-aware logic built on its parser, not an off-the-shelf generic replacement feature.

The Python harness currently imports PyMuPDF for diagnostics and validation even when the candidate mutation uses PDFium/qpdf. Therefore the **current harness is not a tested permissively licensed production-only pipeline**. Replacing those dependencies in the runtime path is part of the proposed next milestone; keep the AGPL reference verifier out of a proprietary distributed application unless appropriately licensed.

## Selection and licensing gates

Adopt **PDFium as the candidate core**, with qpdf as an optional structural validator/companion and application-owned conservative editing policy. Retain PyMuPDF as a research verifier until the application licensing choice is made. This choice preserves a plausible no-paid-API, offline, open-or-closed-source path while acknowledging higher implementation effort than a high-level commercial editor.

For AGPL distribution, confirm compatible source/notice obligations for the integrated application; commercial use and closed-source use are different questions. Process separation is a maintainability choice and is not a legal workaround. For closed-source distribution, either omit copyleft components that require incompatible obligations or obtain suitable commercial rights. pikepdf's MPL obligations differ from AGPL's and should not be conflated.

Before shipping, build a bill of materials from the exact native artifacts, collect required license texts/notices and source offers/access, and audit font embedding/redistribution rights. The Bitstream Vera font used here includes its license in `assets/`. Do not assume that extracting an embedded customer font grants the right to extend or redistribute its full font program. This phase makes no paid purchase and no final product licensing commitment.
